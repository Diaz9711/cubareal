1. First peoples

    Antes de 1492

    Guanahatabeyes, Siboneyes and Taínos

    The island was not empty. Waves arrived from South America and the Caribbean: Guanahatabey foragers, Siboneyes, and later farming Taínos. They grew yuca and maize, made casabe, lived in chiefdoms and left pictographs in caves such as Punta del Este, Isle of Youth. Bartolomé de las Casas estimated hundreds of thousands of Taínos in the Caribbean at the end of the 15th century.

    Source: Pictografías de Punta del Este, Cuba · Britannica (Taíno, Ciboney) · Historia de Cuba

  2. Contact

    1492

    Columbus takes possession of the island

    On October 28, 1492 Christopher Columbus reaches Cuba, on his first voyage, and claims it for Castile. The peoples he finds have names, land and gods. The European map begins. Armed conquest will take almost twenty years.

    Source: Diario del primer viaje · Historia de Cuba

  3. Conquest

    1511

    Velázquez, Hatuey and the collapse of the indigenous peoples

    Diego Velázquez leaves Hispaniola with hundreds of men and founds towns from Baracoa to Havana. The Taíno chief Hatuey, fled from Hispaniola, organizes resistance and is captured and burned alive. Cuban historians describe massacres such as Pánfilo de Narváez’s as genocide. Of about 100,000 indigenous people around 1511, fewer than 5,000 remain by mid-century: war, encomienda, smallpox and suicide.

    Source: Monumento a Hatuey, Baracoa · Torres-Cuevas / Loyola Vega · Wikipedia History of Cuba

  4. Slavery

    1518–1867

    More than a million enslaved Africans

    With the Taínos decimated, Spain introduces the African trade from 1518. Cuba becomes a sugar mill. An Anglo-Spanish treaty of 1817 promised to cut the traffic; planters kept importing. More than 600,000 Africans arrived in the 19th century, most after 1820. The trade did not stop until 1867. Cuba was one of the last slave markets in the West.

    Source: Grabado del azúcar, s. XVIII · Slavery in Cuba · Corwin, Spain and the Abolition of Slavery in Cuba

  5. Independence

    1868

    The Cry of Yara and the first war

    Carlos Manuel de Céspedes frees his slaves at La Demajagua and declares independence. The Ten Years’ War begins (1868–1878). Independence and the end of slavery are born together. The Pact of Zanjón ends the war without the republic they dreamed of.

    Source: Guerra de los Diez Años · Historia de Cuba

  6. Abolition

    1886

    Legal slavery ends

    On October 7, 1886 a Spanish decree ends the patronato: the last 25,000 patrocinados are free. Cuba is among the last territories in the Americas to abolish slavery, only before Brazil (1888). Legal freedom did not erase racism or the plantation.

    Source: Real decreto 7 oct. 1886 · Abolición de la esclavitud en España

  7. Independence

    1895

    Martí, the mambises and 1898

    José Martí organizes the war of 1895 and dies at Dos Ríos on May 19. Maceo and Gómez cross the island. In 1898 the Spanish-American War breaks out: Spain leaves, Washington enters. Cuba was not born fully free: it was born occupied.

    Source: Retrato de José Martí · Guerra de Independencia · Wikipedia

  8. Republic

    1902

    A republic with the Platt Amendment

    On May 20, 1902 the Republic is born. The Platt Amendment (1901) gives the United States the right to intervene. There are schools, sugar and a middle class; also corruption, Machado and, in 1952, Fulgencio Batista’s coup. It is not paradise. It is not yet 67 years of one party.

    Source: Enmienda Platt · Historia de Cuba

  9. Dictatorship

    1959

    The revolution takes power

    On January 1 Batista flees. Fidel Castro enters Havana. The promised elections were never held. The media pass to the state. Executions and prisons for opponents begin. In 1961 the socialist character is declared. One party keeps the country.

    Source: Historia de Cuba · HRW

  10. Dictatorship

    1965–68

    UMAP: forced-labor camps

    Military Units to Aid Production lock up the religious, gay men, Jehovah’s Witnesses and the “disaffected.” Farm labor under military watch. It is not an exile rumor: it is documented by testimony, historians and human-rights groups. The state called it “aid.” It was prison.

    Source: HRW · testimonios UMAP · historiografía cubana independiente

  11. Exodus

    1980

    Mariel: 125,000 leave

    More than 125,000 Cubans leave through the port of Mariel. The government mixes families seeking freedom with common prisoners to stain the exodus. Anyone who wants to leave is treated as trash. The pattern will repeat: the state chooses who stays and whom it expels.

    Source: Mariel boatlift · archivos contemporáneos

  12. Hunger

    1991–94

    The Special Period

    The USSR and the subsidy fall. 16-hour blackouts, optic neuropathy from malnutrition, transport stopped. In 1994 more than 35,000 rafters take to the sea. The people pay for the experiment. The elite does not go hungry.

    Source: OPS / estudios de neuropatía · éxodo de los balseros

  13. Repression

    2003

    The Black Spring

    75 journalists, librarians and opponents sent to prison for up to 28 years. Orlando Zapata Tamayo would die in 2010 after 85 days on hunger strike. Dissent is not a common crime: it is the crime of speaking.

    Source:

  14. July 11

    2021

    “Patria y Vida”

    The largest protest since 1959. Gas, clubs, mass arrests and sentences of up to 25 years, including minors. The 2022 Penal Code criminalizes protest and “fake news.” Any criticism can be a crime.

    Source:

  15. Today

    2024–26

    Hunger, blackouts, 1,339 prisoners

    From 142 political prisoners in 2019 to 1,339 in August 2026 (Prisoners Defenders). 94% in extreme poverty. Eight in ten skip a meal (OCDH). The informal dollar is above 700 pesos. Only 30% of the basic drug list is available. The street drug el químico is in the neighborhood. The eighth nationwide blackout of 2026. Sixty-seven years, one party. The people’s need is not an accident. It is the system.

    Source: Prisoners Defenders ago. 2026 · OCDH · UNE / MINEM 18 sept. 2026 · elTOQUE / Yadio
