The businesses

GAESA is not one hotel. It is the army’s holding company. The Miami Herald, on August 6, 2025, published internal statements for March and August 2024: about $18 billion in current assets, $14.5 billion of it in unidentified banks and in GAESA’s own finance houses. That figure does not include CIMEX’s dollars, so the pile is likely larger. The government denies it. Some economists read the same leak differently. The company names are not in dispute.

  • Gaviota

    Armed Forces hotels and tourism. In March 2024, $8.5 billion sitting in accounts.

  • CIMEX

    Stores, gas stations and the commercial holding. The biggest profit in the leaked quarter.

  • FINCIMEX

    Remittances and cards. The dollar a family sends passes through here.

  • TRD Caribe

    Hard-currency stores. $3.4 billion on the books that March.

  • Almacenes Universales

    Logistics and the port of Mariel. $1.6 billion. Sanctioned in June 2026.

  • Aerogaviota

    The military’s tourist airline.

  • Almest

    The developer that puts up the hotels.

  • RAFIN and BFI

    The group’s own bank and finance house. That is where the cash sits.

The Miami Herald published the leaked statements (Aug 6, 2025): in the first quarter of 2024 the group posted $2.1 billion in net profit. CIMEX alone: $1.2 billion in profit on $3.4 billion in revenue. On the books that March: Gaviota $8.5 billion, TRD Caribe $3.4 billion, Almacenes Universales $1.6 billion. The same documents say the hard-currency businesses do not pay tax. On June 23, 2026 the U.S. Treasury put Almacenes Universales, Banco Financiero Internacional, Rafin, Geominera and Antillana de Acero on the sanctions list.

On the island the wage is written in the Official Gazette. Resolution 17/2026 of the Labor Ministry, special edition of July 28, in force since July 1: a newly graduated doctor earns 7,045 pesos a month. A second-degree specialist earns 8,880. A night hour for a doctor is paid at 50 pesos. At an informal rate above 700 pesos to the dollar, the new doctor’s month is under 11 dollars, and the night hour is under 10 cents. Abroad, the State sells that same doctor. Prisoners Defenders’ medical-brigade archive (June 2026): 53 active missions, about 25,724 professionals — some 13,000 in Venezuela, 3,849 in Mexico, about 3,000 in Angola, 955 in Algeria. Across the contracts they reviewed, the professional kept about 15% and about 85% stayed with the State or a middleman. In Mexico, about $200 of $3,750. The average net pay they declared was $462 a month, on workweeks of nearly 72 hours. In Calabria, Italy, €4,700 a month: €3,500 to Comercializadora de Servicios Médicos Cubanos, €1,200 to the doctor. Bahamas contracts reviewed by Cuba Archive: a specialist billed at up to $12,000, of which the doctor received $1,200 — the State kept as much as 91.6%. The New York Times (April 14, 2025), citing Cuba Archive: the export brings in more than $4 billion a year, and activists put the doctor’s share between 2% and 15%. The Inter-American Commission (resolution of December 31, 2025) wrote down wage withholdings, a hostile workplace and kept passports. On October 4, 2026 Diario de Cuba reported that doctors on the Mexico mission said they had gone more than two months unpaid. In April 2025 Díaz-Canel said they go by conviction and are not forced. The government denies that this is forced labor. The contracts, where they have been read, say who gets paid.

The wars

Syria, 1973

On October 6, 2026 the Cuban ambassador in Egypt told the paper Al Nahar what his own government did in the October 1973 war. Havana sent a brigade of tank crews and advisers to the Syrian front: 746 fighters, posted on the Golan Heights, and they stayed until the end of 1975. That number is not an enemy cable. It is the ambassador’s.

What is not a check

There is no public ledger that shows Havana wiring money to Hamas or Hezbollah. What is written is something else. A February 27, 2009 cable from the U.S. Interests Section in Havana, published by WikiLeaks, said it had reports that FARC and ELN members rested in Cuba and received medical care. In 2019, 2021 and 2023 Cuban diplomats in Beirut met representatives of Hamas and Hezbollah; a 2025 report cited by Martí Noticias records those meetings. A meeting is not a transfer. This page does not turn a visit into a bank draft nobody published.

Ukraine

In Ukraine the count is not closed. A State Department report sent to Congress, and described by Axios on April 14, 2026, puts between 1,000 and 5,000 Cubans fighting on Russia’s side at any one time. Ukraine’s military intelligence said on October 15, 2025 that it had identified 1,076, and that 96 were dead or missing. On March 31, 2026 that same service estimated the number recruited could reach 10,000 or 15,000. That is an estimate, not a census. They go into assault companies after about two weeks of training. Havana says it uncovered an illegal network and did not send them. Washington’s report says the public file does not prove the regime dispatched every one of them, and that there are signs it tolerated or helped the departures. The 2024 trafficking report said the government was involved or, at minimum, aware.
